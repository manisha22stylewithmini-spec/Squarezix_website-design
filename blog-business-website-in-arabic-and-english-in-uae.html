Should My Business Website Be in Both Arabic and English in UAE?

By Squarezix TeamJuly 30, 202615 mins read

Yes. Most UAE businesses should have a website available in both Arabic and English.

A bilingual website helps businesses:

  • Reach both Arabic and English-speaking audiences
  • Improve local SEO performance
  • Build credibility with UAE customers
  • Increase enquiries and sales
  • Provide a better customer experience
  • Prepare for AI-powered search platforms like Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity
  • Strengthen brand authority across diverse customer segments

The key, however, is implementing a bilingual website correctly. Simply translating English content into Arabic using automated tools is not enough. A successful bilingual website requires professional localization, proper technical SEO, right-to-left (RTL) support, and culturally appropriate messaging.

Why UAE Businesses Are Different from Other Markets

The UAE is one of the world’s most multicultural business environments. Unlike many countries where a single language dominates online communication, the UAE serves audiences with different linguistic preferences.

Your potential customers may include:

  • Emirati citizens
  • Arabic-speaking residents
  • International expatriates
  • GCC visitors
  • Tourists
  • Investors
  • Corporate decision-makers
  • Government organizations

Each audience searches differently, consumes information differently, and often prefers interacting in their native language.

For example, an English-speaking entrepreneur may search for:

  • Website development company in Dubai
  • Best accounting services in Dubai

Meanwhile, an Arabic-speaking customer may search for equivalent services using Arabic keywords.

If your website only serves one language, you’re effectively limiting your visibility to a significant portion of your potential audience.

Does a Bilingual Website Improve SEO?

Yes, but only when implemented correctly.

Many business owners assume that translating a website automatically doubles their rankings. In reality, bilingual SEO requires a strategic approach.

Google treats Arabic and English pages as separate content assets.

This creates opportunities to rank for different search queries in each language while serving the most relevant content to users based on their language preferences.

A professionally optimized bilingual website can increase visibility for:

  • English keywords
  • Arabic keywords
  • Local searches
  • Voice searches
  • Mobile searches
  • AI-generated search experiences

However, achieving these benefits requires proper technical implementation, language-specific keyword research, optimized metadata, and localized content—not just direct translation. This can be achieved by partnering with an seo agency in Dubai.

Translation vs. Localization: They Are Not the Same

One of the biggest misconceptions about creating a Business Website in Arabic and English is believing that translating text is enough. While translation converts words from one language to another, localization adapts your website to the cultural expectations, search behavior, and user preferences of your target audience.

For example, an English call-to-action such as “Book a Free Consultation” may not resonate with Arabic-speaking visitors in the same way. A professionally localized version considers cultural nuances, reading patterns, tone, and user expectations while maintaining your brand’s identity.

Localization also includes:

  • Using culturally appropriate imagery
  • Adapting tone and messaging
  • Displaying dates and numbers correctly
  • Supporting Arabic right-to-left (RTL) layouts
  • Optimizing content for Arabic search intent
  • Writing unique metadata for each language

A localized website feels as though it was originally created for Arabic-speaking users, rather than simply translated from English. This creates a stronger connection with visitors and often leads to better engagement and higher conversion rates.

Technical SEO Best Practices for Bilingual Websites

Many bilingual websites fail to rank because they overlook technical SEO fundamentals.

To maximize visibility, your website should include the following:

1. Hreflang Tags

Hreflang tags tell search engines which version of a page to display based on a user’s language and region.

This helps prevent duplicate content issues and ensures users land on the correct language version.

2. Language-Specific URLs

A clean URL structure improves usability and SEO.

Recommended format:

example.com/
example.com/ar/

This approach keeps both languages under the same domain, making website management and authority building more efficient.

Avoid mixing languages on a single page or relying solely on browser translation.

3. Separate XML Sitemaps

Include both Arabic and English pages in your XML sitemap so search engines can discover and index every version efficiently.

4. Right-to-Left (RTL) Support

Arabic is written from right to left.

Simply reversing text direction is not enough.

A professional RTL implementation includes:

  • Correct menu alignment
  • Proper spacing
  • Optimized typography
  • RTL-friendly forms
  • Responsive layouts
  • Consistent visual hierarchy

Poor RTL implementation can make a website difficult to navigate and reduce user confidence.

5. Language-Specific Metadata

Each language requires its own:

  • Meta title
  • Meta description
  • Open Graph tags
  • Schema markup (where applicable)
  • Image alt text

This helps search engines understand each version independently and improves click-through rates from search results.

6. Optimized Internal Linking

Your English pages should naturally link to relevant English content, while Arabic pages should connect to related Arabic pages.

A clear internal linking structure (on page optimization) improves crawlability and encourages users to explore more of your website.

7. Fast Loading Performance

A bilingual website should remain fast regardless of language.

Optimize:

  • Images
  • Fonts
  • CSS and JavaScript
  • Caching
  • Content Delivery Networks (CDNs)

Fast-loading websites improve both user experience and search performance.

Common Mistakes Businesses Make

Many companies invest in bilingual websites but fail to achieve the expected results because of avoidable mistakes.

Using Automatic Translation Only

Machine-translated pages often sound unnatural and may not reflect your brand’s professionalism.

Ignoring Arabic Keyword Research

Users search differently in Arabic than in English.

Successful bilingual SEO requires independent keyword research for each language.

Forgetting Mobile Optimization

Most UAE users browse websites on smartphones.

Ensure that both Arabic and English versions provide the same seamless mobile experience.

Missing Language Switcher

Visitors should be able to switch languages easily from any page without restarting their journey.

The language selector should be visible, intuitive, and preserve the equivalent page whenever possible.

Duplicate Metadata

Using the same title and description across languages reduces SEO effectiveness.

Each page should have language-specific metadata that reflects how users search.

Poor Content Synchronization

Updating only the English version while leaving Arabic pages outdated creates inconsistency and reduces trust.

Establish a workflow to keep both language versions current whenever services, pricing, or company information changes.

Arabic SEO Is Different from English SEO

One of the most overlooked aspects of multilingual websites is assuming that SEO works the same in every language.

It doesn’t.

Arabic-speaking users often use different search patterns, phrasing, and terminology than English-speaking audiences.

For example, the most searched Arabic keyword may not be a direct translation of the highest-volume English keyword.

Effective Arabic SEO involves:

  • Dedicated keyword research
  • Native-language content writing
  • Optimized headings
  • Local search intent analysis
  • Region-specific terminology
  • High-quality internal linking
  • Structured content for featured snippets

Businesses that invest in proper Arabic SEO gain access to an additional audience that many competitors overlook.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it mandatory for businesses in the UAE to have an Arabic website?

There is no universal legal requirement for every private business to have an Arabic website. However, businesses serving local customers, government entities, or regulated sectors often benefit significantly from providing Arabic content.

Does an Arabic website improve Google rankings?

An Arabic website doesn’t automatically improve rankings, but it creates additional opportunities to rank for Arabic-language searches and reach a wider audience when supported by proper SEO.

Can I translate my existing website into Arabic?

Yes. Existing websites can be converted into bilingual websites. However, professional localization, Arabic SEO, and proper technical implementation will produce far better results than simple machine translation.

Should Arabic and English pages have different URLs?

Yes. Using separate URLs—such as /ar/ for Arabic pages—is considered a best practice because it helps search engines index each language version correctly.

What is RTL and why is it important?

RTL stands for Right-to-Left, the writing direction used by Arabic. A properly implemented RTL layout ensures Arabic users enjoy the same intuitive experience as English-speaking visitors.

Will a bilingual website help my business appear in AI search results?

High-quality bilingual content improves your ability to answer user queries in multiple languages, making your website more valuable to AI-powered search platforms such as Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity.

How long does it take to build a bilingual website?

The timeline depends on the number of pages, functionality, content creation, and translation requirements. A professionally developed bilingual website typically takes longer than a single-language website because each language version requires testing and optimization.

Can I add Arabic to my website later?

Yes. However, planning for multilingual functionality from the beginning is usually more cost-effective and results in a better user experience.
